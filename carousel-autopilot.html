01

What the playbook actually builds

Strip the sales pages and the 39-page prompt appendix, and roughly 30 pages of real system remains. It is sound, and the architecture is worth keeping.

Layer 1
Sense

Pull trends from IG hashtags, YouTube Data API, X v2. Score by (likes + 3·comments + 5·shares) / reach. Keep the top 15–20%, drop anything over 48h old.

Layer 2
Think

GPT writes caption + hooks as JSON. Claude expands that into one image prompt per slide, all sharing a fixed "visual DNA" block.

Layer 3
Make

generate.py loops the prompt list through an image API in a 3-worker pool, writes a .prompt.txt beside every PNG.

Layer 4
Ship

Meta Graph API publish at a randomised time inside a daily window; ManyChat or a native webhook turns keyword comments into DMs.

Three files per dated folder — content.py (filename/prompt tuples), generate.py (batch render), build_pdfs.py (the same content as a PDF) — copied rather than abstracted, twenty-plus times. The claimed result is @zero.canon_ passing 10k followers in under 30 days with no manual posting.

What genuinely earns a place in your build

  • The four-layer split. Sense / Think / Make / Ship are independently replaceable. That is the right decomposition and you should keep the names.
  • Reach-normalised engagement scoring. Weighting comments and shares above likes, then dividing by reach, is correct: it lets a small account's high-rate post outrank a big account's loud one.
  • One frozen style constant, repeated per slide. Consistency across a 10-slide carousel comes from repetition, not from one longer prompt. This is the single best idea in the document.
  • A saved prompt beside every rendered asset. Cheap, and it is the difference between debugging a bad slide in one minute versus thirty.
  • Batch, not daemon. One scheduled run a day. Continuous polling buys nothing — trends do not move hourly.
  • Randomised posting slot. A fixed 09:00:00 every day is the most legible automation signature there is.
02

Where it is wrong, stale, or silent

Six findings. The first three will each stop a first build dead, and none of them are mentioned anywhere in the 74 pages.

SeverityFindingWhat is actually true
Blocker It never shows how to publish a carousel. Section 13 teaches the two-call single-image flow only. A carousel is a three-call flow: one child container per slide with is_carousel_item=true, then a parent container with media_type=CAROUSEL and a children list, then publish the parent. Max 10 slides via the API (the app allows 20). Every container expires after 24h.
Blocker Nothing says where the PNGs are hosted. The pipeline writes files to a local folder, then the publish call passes image_url. Meta fetches that URL from its own servers. Local paths, file://, and private buckets all fail. You need a public HTTPS URL — S3/R2 with public read, or Cloudinary. And Instagram wants JPEG: the playbook's whole pipeline emits PNG.
Blocker Facebook is absent. The word appears only as "the Page you must link to Instagram." Page posting is a different flow: upload each photo to /{page-id}/photos with published=false, then POST /{page-id}/feed with an attached_media array of media_fbid values. Different token (Page token), different scopes (pages_manage_posts, pages_read_engagement), same 24h expiry on the unpublished photos.
Stale "A hard cap of 50 published posts per account per 24 hours." The content-publishing limit is 100 per rolling 24h, enforced at media_publish, not at container creation. A carousel counts as one.
Overstated "App Review commonly takes 2–4 weeks and is the biggest bottleneck — put it in week one." Advanced Access exists to serve other people's accounts. To publish to accounts that hold a role on your own app — which is your case — Standard Access is normally enough. Confirm in App Dashboard → App Review → Permissions before you plan a month around it.
Design Headlines, body copy, footers and slide counters are all drawn by an image model. Its own production prompts beg — "Perfect spelling, no garbled glyphs", "NO lorem ipsum" — which is the tell. You cannot version a brand this way, cannot fix a typo without a re-roll, and pay per attempt. Section 04 below replaces it.

Two smaller ones

Two vendors for one voice. GPT writes the caption, Claude writes the slide prompts. Nothing requires the split, and it guarantees the caption and the slides drift apart in tone. One model, one call, one object.

The loop never closes. Sense reads the whole internet and never reads your own account. Post 200 times and the ranking function is exactly as smart as on day one. That is the difference between automation and a compounding system.

03

The build, in eight phases

Same four layers, Claude-native, both platforms, deterministic rendering. Phases 0–3 are a weekend; 4–5 are a second weekend; 6–7 are what makes it improve on its own.

00Accounts
& tokens

Get the account chain and a token that does not expire

Instagram account → Professional (Business). Facebook Page → linked to it. Meta app of type Business at developers.facebook.com, with Instagram Graph API added. Verify in Graph API Explorer that /me/accounts?fields=instagram_business_account returns your IG user ID before writing a line of code.

Then skip the playbook's 60-day-token treadmill entirely. In Meta Business Suite, create a System User, assign it the Page and IG asset, and generate a token with instagram_business_content_publish, pages_manage_posts, pages_read_engagement, instagram_manage_comments. System user tokens do not expire. The playbook's single most common silent-failure mode — "an expired token is why your job quietly stopped posting" — stops existing.

01Brand
kernel

Write the brand once, as data

One brand/kernel.md (voice, audience, what you will and will not claim, three worked example captions) and one brand/tokens.json (hex values, type scale, safe margins, logo rules, slide dimensions). Everything downstream — the Claude system prompt, the slide CSS, the QA linter — reads from these two files and nowhere else.

This is the playbook's "shared visual DNA" idea, promoted from a string pasted inside a Python file to the actual source of truth. Change the accent hex in one place and the next drop is on-brand end to end.

02Sense

Read your own account before you read the internet

Keep their scoring formula and their dated raw-JSON cache. Add one source they never mention: /{ig-user-id}/media?fields=caption,like_count,comments_count,insights.metric(reach,saved,shares). Your last 60 posts, pulled nightly into data/history.jsonl.

Start with YouTube (free 10k-unit daily quota) and your own insights. Add X only once the account is earning — the v2 Basic tier is a real subscription for a marginal signal.

Normalise everything to one shape before scoring — {platform, topic, url, likes, comments, shares, reach, published_at} — so the ranking function never branches on source.

03Think

One Claude call returns the entire post

Not a caption call and a prompt call. One call, one validated object: hook, 8–10 slides each with headline / body / optional visual brief, caption, first comment, hashtags, alt text per slide, and a Facebook variant of the caption. Structured output means it either validates or it fails loudly — no regex over a paragraph.

The kernel + tokens + winners block is identical on every run, so it sits before the cache breakpoint and the volatile brief goes after it. Watch usage.cache_read_input_tokens — if it stays at zero, something in your "frozen" prefix is not frozen, and a timestamp is the usual culprit.

Route by difficulty: Opus 5 writes the post; Haiku 4.5 does the cheap mechanical passes (dedupe checks, hashtag hygiene, alt text). Do not pay flagship rates for classification.

04Make

Render the slides in HTML, screenshot them, and never ask a model to spell

This is the change that does the most work. A slide is an HTML template reading brand/tokens.json; Playwright loads it at 1080×1350 and screenshots to JPEG. Real fonts, real kerning, exact hex, perfect spelling, identical footer on every slide, deterministic and free.

The image model keeps a real job, just a smaller one: backgrounds and hero objects, generated once per drop and composited behind the HTML text layer as a CSS background. Textures and product shots are what diffusion is good at. Typography is not.

Second-order wins that fall out of this for free: a slide costs nothing to re-render after a copy fix; the same template exports 1080×1920 for Stories and 1080×1080 for a Facebook square by changing the viewport; and your carousel can contain a real chart or a real code block, which no image model will ever render correctly.

05Ship

Upload once, publish twice

Push the JPEGs to a public bucket (R2 or S3, public-read, one prefix per drop). Then Instagram — three calls — and Facebook — two.

Keep their randomised slot — a uniform offset inside a 07:00–21:00 window, seeded separately per account — and add an idempotency key per drop so a retry can never double-post.

06Gate

One human keystroke, and a linter that does not need one

Before anything publishes, run a deterministic check: spelling, banned claims, character budget per slide (does the headline overflow its box at the rendered size?), hex values matching tokens, hashtag count, alt text present, topic not posted in the trailing 45 days. Fail closed.

Then, for the first month, a review queue — the rendered JPEGs and the caption pushed to yourself, approve or reject. The playbook's premise is zero human touches from day one; that is how a brand ships something embarrassing at 3am. Drop the gate to spot-checks once the linter has caught nothing for two weeks.

07Learn

Feed results back into the brief

72 hours after each post, pull its insights and append the result to data/history.jsonl: hook text, format, topic, slide count, publish hour, saves, shares, reach, follows. Every subsequent Think call receives your top 20 and bottom 5 as part of the cached prefix, labelled.

This is the only part of the system that compounds. Everything else is throughput.

04

The improvements, ranked by what they return

Ordered by leverage, not effort. The top four are where the multiple actually lives — the rest are the difference between a system that runs and one you can leave alone.

01Move typography out of the image modelHTML template + Playwright screenshot instead of diffusion-drawn textHighest

Perfect spelling by construction. Pixel-identical branding across every slide of every drop. A copy fix costs a re-render, not a re-roll and a new charge. Charts, tables and code blocks become possible slide content. Near-zero marginal cost per slide, versus per-image API pricing.

It also kills the failure mode the playbook itself flags as unfixable — that a general image model drifts on product accuracy across a batch — without buying the subscription it recommends as the workaround.

02Close the loop on your own analyticsRank tomorrow's ideas using what your audience actually saved and sharedHighest

The playbook optimises for what is trending globally. You want what converts for you. Track per post: hook pattern, topic cluster, slide count, publish hour, saves, shares, profile visits, follows. After 30 posts you can rank hooks by realised save-rate instead of by guess; after 90 the ranking is genuinely yours and cannot be copied by anyone reading the same playbook.

03One story object, many outputsFan out from a single Think pass instead of re-prompting per formatHigh

The expensive step is deciding what to say. Once you have the post plan, the marginal cost of a Facebook variant, a 1080×1920 Story cut, a Reel cover, a LinkedIn version and a newsletter section is a different template and a different viewport — no extra reasoning. The playbook writes one post for one platform per day. Same spend, five surfaces.

04Cache the kernel, batch the backlogPrompt caching on the frozen prefix; Batch API for anything not needed todayHigh

Your brand kernel, tokens and winners block are identical on every call and are the bulk of the input. Behind a cache breakpoint they are read at a fraction of input price. Separately, anything generated ahead of schedule — a 30-post evergreen backfill, a month of variants — goes through the Batch API at 50% of standard cost, since nothing needs a same-second response.

Combined with Haiku 4.5 on the mechanical passes, the Think layer lands at a few dollars a month rather than a few tens.

05A brand linter that fails closedDeterministic checks before publish, not model self-assessmentMedium

Spelling, banned claims, per-slide character budget against the rendered box, hex conformance to tokens, hashtag count, alt text present, no topic repeat inside 45 days. Written as ordinary code with tests, not as "please check your work" in a prompt. A model asked to grade itself will pass itself.

06Semantic dedupe, not string dedupeEmbed every shipped topic; block on similarity, not on exact matchMedium

The playbook skips a topic already posted in the trailing 14 days. "AI agents for founders" and "how founders should use AI agents" are different strings and the same post. Embed each shipped topic into a local vector file and block anything above a similarity threshold. Fourteen days is also too short — 45 is closer to when an audience forgets.

07A hook bank with recorded outcomesGenerate five, ship one, log which pattern wonMedium

The playbook already asks for three alternative hooks and then discards two. Keep them. Tag each by pattern — contrarian, numbered, question, result-first, mistake — ship the highest-ranked, and record the outcome against the pattern. Within two months you know which openings your audience stops for, which is the highest-value fact the whole system can learn.

08A post is a state machine, not a log linedraft → approved → uploaded → containers_created → published → measuredMedium

"Log every publish attempt" tells you what broke; it does not stop the damage. One row per drop with an explicit status, an idempotency key, and the container IDs, means a crashed run resumes exactly where it stopped and a retry after a timeout cannot post twice. Containers expire at 24h — the state machine is what notices.

09A non-expiring token and a daily health checkSystem User token, plus a cheap call that proves it still worksMedium

Removes the playbook's own nominated failure mode. Keep the health check anyway — a permission can be revoked, an asset can be unassigned, a Page role can change. One GET /me a day and an alert on failure beats discovering it a week later.

10An evergreen re-render engineYour top 10% of posts, re-cut with new visuals, on a 60-day cycleMedium

Because slides are templates and text is data, a winning post from two months ago re-renders into a new visual treatment for the cost of a screenshot. Almost none of your audience saw the original — reach on any single post is a small fraction of followers. This is free inventory that only exists once rendering is deterministic.

11Reply generation with a human in the loopDraft comment replies; never auto-DM outside the policy windowMedium

Comment-to-DM is where accounts get restricted, and the playbook is thin on it. Keep keyword-triggered delivery strictly inside Meta's messaging window, do not send unsolicited DMs at volume, and have Claude draft replies to real comments for one-tap approval rather than posting them unread. Early conversations are worth more than early throughput.

12Run it as one repo with tests, not twenty copied foldersA library plus dated data, instead of a copied three-file patternLow

Copy-a-folder-per-drop is honest and it works to about twenty. Past that, a bug fixed in generate.py exists in one of twenty-three copies. Move the pipeline into src/ with tests; keep dated folders for data — brief, plan JSON, rendered slides, publish receipt, measured results. You keep the auditability and drop the duplication.

Two things to keep in view

Publishing through the Graph API is the sanctioned route and is not the risk — automated DMs at volume are. Stay inside Meta's messaging window, keep delivery keyword-triggered, and treat the reply loop as the part of this system that can cost you the account. Separately: an account that posts daily and never answers a comment reads as automated to humans long before it does to a platform. Budget ten minutes a day for the replies; it is the only manual step worth keeping.
